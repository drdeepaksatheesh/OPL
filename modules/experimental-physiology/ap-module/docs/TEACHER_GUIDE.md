# Instructor's guide — action potential & its properties

**Target:** first-year MBBS / BSc Nursing, adaptable to postgraduate neurophysiology. **Suggested duration:** 50–65 min. **Learning sequence:** phenomenon → method → observation → explanation → experiment requiring repeated trials.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, students should be able to:

1. Distinguish Vm (intracellular potential) from stimulus current and a stimulus artifact; read correct units, references and axes.
2. Locate quiet resting baseline, an operational threshold, rising limb, positive overshoot (when present), repolarization, AHP and stable recovery in a suitable single-spike recording.
3. Measure Vm baseline, Vm peak, AP amplitude (= Vpeak − Vrest), duration using a stated definition, stimulus-to-peak timing (NOT initiation latency) and AHP depth with documented uncertainty.
4. State why all-or-none requires a controlled stimulus series in the same axon/fiber, and why compound AP/muscle responses can grow through recruitment.
5. Describe stimulus duration versus intensity; rheobase, chronaxie and why exact estimates require multiple trials.
6. Explain absolute and relative refractory periods using controlled paired-pulse experiments rather than a single graph.
7. State why propagation/velocity requires at least two known recording sites or another validated spatial timing protocol.
8. Recognize that slowly rising depolarization (accommodation), repetitive firing and frequency adaptation are distinct properties, not simply phases.

## A 60-minute activity

- **0–8 min:** Ask what the experimenter recorded. Draw two axes separately: current input and membrane voltage. Inspect stimulus timing line versus rectangular pulse.
- **8–22 min:** Phase plot (explicitly modelled); students label membrane potential, operational threshold, overshoot, AHP. Compute the example AP amplitude: +32 − (−70) = **102 mV**. Stress this is an illustrative calculation.
- **22–30 min:** Open the source-attributed mouse hippocampal recording and Olsen's published AHP recordings. Ask whether current is OFF at baseline recovery and whether scale marks are adequate for measurement.
- **30–42 min:** Properties tab. For each property, students choose required experimental manipulation before reading its answer.
- **42–52 min:** Original SGAMP a1t18 dual-channel excerpt. Compare finite 1-ms stimulus with the textbook timing mark; identify repetitive firing and distinguish a post-spike trough from a stable resting baseline.
- **52–60 min:** Five formative questions plus one short written protocol: design an all-or-none test in an individual axon versus a whole nerve.

## Reference points and common errors

- Resting −70 mV and threshold −55 mV are **examples**. Cells have variable resting potential and threshold; a threshold estimate depends on dV/dt, noise and the protocol.
- In a normal mammalian neuron, fast rising/falling phases are commonly associated with voltage-gated Na+ and K+ currents. Shape alone does not identify the channels; voltage clamp/pharmacology does.
- AHP below rest is distinct from the baseline maintained during continued depolarizing current.
- Positive peak potential is not amplitude; zero mV crossing is not the same as threshold.
- A 1-ms current stimulus drawn with finite width is not an artifact; a line can be a conventional onset marker.
- “All-or-none” refers to regenerative excitation of one element under comparable conditions, NOT fixed compound response amplitude regardless of the number of recruited axons or fibers.
- A refractory period is not a visual segment of the AP; it is measured using a response to a second stimulus.
- A single-site Vm recording cannot measure conduction velocity.

## Suggested independent student deliverables

A. One calibrated original tracing with raw-source citation, scaling, chosen baseline interval and approximate peak, accompanied by uncertainty and annotation method. 
B. A protocol sketch showing subthreshold/near-threshold/suprathreshold current injections.
C. An original paired-pulse protocol stating pulse timing, expected responses and possible confounding drift. 
D. A short comparison between model, published figure and calibrated original waveform.

## Evidence grades

**M — Modelled:** useful to visualize a concept, NEVER evidence of recorded physiology. **F — Published figure:** authentic trace in publication, waveform samples may be unavailable. **R — verified raw derivative:** original master/provenance/citation/calibration and checksums checked. For claims requiring multiple trials, the grade remains inadequate even if one genuine recording is R.
